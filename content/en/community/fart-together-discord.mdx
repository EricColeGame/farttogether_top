## Why the Fart Together Discord Matters Right Now

Fart Together is a physics-based co-op game where two to four players steer one shared raft using fart power, and it needs coordinated teammates more than it needs solo practice. That makes the official Fart Together Discord the fastest place to find people who will actually fart on the same beat as you. The server's invite page describes it as the official community hub where players try the demo, wishlist the game on Steam, find a crew, share funny fails, and take on community challenges.

The game is developed and published by GamesMrkt, a small independent studio that has been building games since 2018 and calls this its first Steam release. Early Access is planned for October 29, 2026, and at the time this article was written the store page showed the unlock happening within hours. Early Access is expected to last roughly two to three months, though the studio notes that estimate can shift with development progress and player feedback.

## What Happens Inside the Official Fart Together Discord

The invite page's own description sets the tone: play the demo, wishlist the game, find your crew, share your funniest fails, take on community challenges, and join the chaos. That short list maps neatly onto how the game is meant to be played — with other people, loudly, and with a lot of blame being passed around.

- **It complements Steam, not replaces it.** The developers say they collect feedback through Steam discussions, their Discord community, and player surveys.

## How to Join the Fart Together Discord Server

There's no secret handshake. The invite is public, and the process takes about a minute.

1. **Open the invite.** Use the official invite at [discord.com/invite/farttogether](https://discord.com/invite/farttogether), or reach it through the Links & info section of the [Fart Together page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/).
2. **Sign in or create a Discord account.** You'll need a standard account to accept any invite.
3. **Accept the invite** and read whatever onboarding the server presents. Rules and channel layouts aren't described on the invite page, so read what's actually there rather than assuming.
4. **Grab the demo** from Steam if you haven't already, so you can actually play when a group forms.
5. **Introduce yourself and look for a crew.** The invite page frames the server around exactly that: finding people to play with.

One caveat worth repeating: because invite links get reposted everywhere, always confirm you're joining through the Steam store page link or the invite URL above. Anyone promising early keys, private builds, or moderator status in exchange for anything is not something the official materials describe.

## Matching the Server to What the Game Actually Asks of You

Fart Together isn't a game where one skilled player can carry the run. The raft responds to the force, direction, and timing of each player, and the developers are blunt about the consequence: one person going the wrong way, reacting late, or firing at the wrong moment can send the whole raft off course. Narrow passages, sharp turns, and moving obstacles turn every course into a timing problem for the group.

That's why a community server is genuinely useful rather than decorative. Random matchmaking gives you strangers; a Discord crew gives you people you can talk to between runs, argue with about "fart direction," and re-queue with after a disaster. The developers even built the marketing around the social friction — the tagline is that the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right time.

## How the Discord Fits Into Early Access Development

GamesMrkt has been specific about why Early Access exists for this game: refining teamwork, physics, controls, and difficulty with players. The studio says community feedback will help it prioritize improvements and future content, and that it plans to share development updates with the community. It also intends to add more courses, obstacles, game modes, characters, and customization options over time, alongside balance and performance work.

That makes the Discord more than a hangout during the Early Access window — it's one of the channels the developers named as an input to their roadmap. If a specific course feels unfair or a control scheme feels muddy, the server and the Steam boards are where that conversation is supposed to happen. The studio has also said Early Access owners will receive the full release at no additional cost, and that pricing is planned to be lower during Early Access and higher at full launch.

On the technical side, online co-op requires a stable internet connection, and the minimum specs listed on Steam start at Windows 10 64-bit, an Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 3 1200, 8 GB of RAM, and a GeForce GTX 1050 Ti or Radeon RX 560. Interface support covers English plus 16 additional languages.
