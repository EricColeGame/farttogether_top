## What "Fart Together Parkour" Actually Means

If you searched for **Fart Together parkour**, you probably pictured a character sprinting, vaulting, and wall-jumping. That's not what this game is. According to the official Steam listing, Fart Together is a physics-based obstacle course game where two to four players steer a single shared raft using fart power. The "parkour" part of the experience is really about precise, coordinated traversal — squeezing through gaps, cutting sharp turns, and dodging moving hazards — rather than running on foot. Understanding that distinction matters, because the skills that make a good run here are timing and communication, not reflexes on a jump button.

The developers at GamesMrkt describe the core loop in one line: the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right time. That single sentence tells you almost everything about how movement works. You can read the full official description on the [Fart Together Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/).

## How Shared-Raft Movement Works

Every player contributes force to the same vehicle. The raft responds to the direction, strength, and timing of each person's input, which means your individual action is never truly individual — it's one vector in a group physics problem.

### Steering With Fart Power

Because all players push the same raft, direction is a negotiation. If one person commits to a direction while the rest are angled elsewhere, the raft doesn't split the difference cleanly; it lurches. The store page frames this as the game's central tension: one player going the wrong way or reacting late can send the whole group off course.

### Timing Beats Brute Force

The developers are explicit that raw power isn't the answer. Courses are built around moments where the team must speed up, slow down, or all commit to the same move at the same instant. A well-timed group burst can rescue a run at the last second; a single mistimed input can turn it into a comedy of errors.

### "Sort of Realistic" Physics

GamesMrkt is refreshingly honest about the simulation. The raft reacts to force, direction, and timing, but the studio openly says it probably shouldn't claim this resembles real raft physics. Expect momentum to feel weighty and readable rather than authentic — enough internal logic that you can argue about why the raft flew sideways, which is apparently part of the fun.

## Obstacle Course Mechanics You'll Face

The Steam listing names the specific hazards you'll navigate, and they map closely to what people mean when they say parkour: tight spaces and moving geometry.

- **Narrow passages** that punish a raft sitting at the wrong angle
- **Sharp turns** that require the whole crew to rotate together
- **Moving obstacles** that force you to wait, then commit
- **Balance hazards** that throw the raft off kilter mid-run

These aren't separate modes or unlockable challenges — they're the vocabulary of the courses themselves. The difficulty comes from combining them: a narrow gap immediately after a turn, or a moving obstacle timed against a section where you need to slow down. Courses are described as linear and top-down in presentation, with a stylized, colorful, family-friendly look that sits oddly well next to the friendship-testing premise.

## Co-op Coordination Is the Real Mechanic

Fart Together is built for two to four players in online co-op, and the store page is blunt that coordination is a requirement rather than a suggestion. The game even warns it may test your friendships — a marketing line, yes, but one that describes genuine design intent.

What makes the coordination interesting is the blame loop. When a run fails, the cause is visible to everyone: someone turned the wrong way, someone fired late, someone hesitated. That transparency is why the developers expect groups to cycle through laughing, shouting, pointing fingers, and eventually saying "one more try." The studio built the game specifically to produce those moments, both for players and for anyone watching.

Practically speaking, that means the most useful thing you can do before your first session is agree on who calls the timing. Voice chat isn't listed as a feature requirement, but a shared callout — "now," "hold," "left" — does the job that four independent sets of reflexes can't.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Fart Together parkour, or something else?**
It's better described as physics-based obstacle-course traversal. The Fart Together parkour comparison comes from the precision movement — tight gaps, sharp turns, timed dodges — but you're steering a shared raft rather than running and jumping on foot.

**How many players can join a run?**
Two to four players in online co-op. Every player controls force on the same raft, so adding people adds coordination difficulty as well as chaos.

**Can one player carry the team?**
Not really. Because the raft responds to everyone's combined direction and timing, an over-eager player is usually the reason a run fails rather than the reason it succeeds. The developers stress synchronized timing over individual power.

**When does it leave Early Access?**
GamesMrkt estimates roughly two to three months, with the caveat that the timeline depends on development progress and player feedback. More courses, modes, and characters are planned for the full release.
