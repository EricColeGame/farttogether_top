One important limit up front: the verified data covers only the **Demo Any%** category. No other category has an established record in the source material, so any claim about a "full game record" or a "solo record" is unconfirmed at this point.

## How the Demo Any% Category Works

If you want to understand the Fart Together world record, you first need to understand what the category actually asks of you. The rules are short and strict:

- **Objective:** finish the demo as quickly as possible. There is no partial-completion scoring and no style bonus.
- **Timing method:** in-game time (IGT). The clock the game tracks is what counts, not your recording software's timer.
- **Team size:** up to 4 players. Runs with fewer players are accepted and ranked on the same board.
- **Proof requirements:** video is mandatory, and every submission must pass verification before it appears on the leaderboard.

That last point is the reason the board is short. A run does not count because you say it happened — it counts because a verifier watched the footage and accepted it.

The full ruleset and live standings live on the [Demo Any% category page](https://www.speedrun.com/Fart_Together?h=demo-any&x=jdz1nv6k).

### Why IGT Instead of Real Time?

In-game time strips out loading screens, lobby waits, and connection hiccups. That matters enormously here, because Fart Together is an online co-op game — the Steam listing explicitly notes that a stable internet connection is required for online play. Real-time timing would punish teams for their router, not their rafting.

## Why Coordination Is the Whole Mechanic

Fart Together is a physics-based obstacle course game from developer and publisher GamesMrkt, where two to four players steer one shared raft using fart power. The raft responds to the **force, direction, and timing** of each individual player's input, so every action ripples across the whole team.

According to the game's own description on [the official Fart Together Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/), the secret is not farting harder — it is farting together at the right moment. Courses feature narrow passages, sharp turns, and moving hazards that knock the raft off balance.

That design is exactly why the Demo Any% record is so tight. A four-player team has more total thrust available, but also more chances for one person to fire a fraction of a second early and send the raft sideways into a wall. The 0.340-second gap between first and second place is the price of one imperfect sync.

## Chasing the Record Yourself

The demo is freely downloadable, and the leaderboard is open to new submissions. Based on the verified rules, here is the honest path to a listed time:

1. **Play the demo until you know the course layout cold.** The category is Any%, so routing knowledge is the baseline — you cannot optimize a course you are still discovering.
2. **Lock in a fixed team.** The top runs used four, three, and two players respectively, so team size is flexible, but voice communication is not optional in a game built around simultaneous inputs.
3. **Practice synchronized pushes.** Since the raft reacts to the timing of every player, drill the moments where the whole team must move at once.
4. **Record every attempt.** Video proof is a hard requirement — a run without footage cannot be verified no matter how fast it was.
5. **Submit through Speedrun.com and wait for verification.** Only verified runs appear on the board.

Be realistic about the bar: 4:08.560 is the number to beat, and the current second-place team missed it by a third of a second.
