In Fart Together, fart mechanics are not just a joke button—they are the propulsion system for a shared raft. The core loop is easy to describe and hard to execute: two to four players each contribute force, direction, and timing to the same vessel, so every blast changes the whole team’s ride. The [official Fart Together Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/) confirms the central rule: success comes from synchronized timing, not from producing the strongest blast.

## How the Shared-Raft Fart Mechanic Works

Fart Together is a physics-based obstacle course game in which 2–4 players steer one raft using fart power. Instead of giving each player a separate vehicle, the game puts everyone on the same platform. That design turns every input into a team-wide event. A blast that might be useful alone can push the group off line when it arrives late, early, or out of sync with everyone else. The Steam description frames the core inputs as direction, timing, and gas power, all managed together.

- **Direction:** Where the raft is pushed. One player going the wrong way can send the entire group off course.
- **Timing:** When the push lands. Reacting too late or blasting at the wrong moment can ruin a maneuver.
- **Gas power:** The strength a player adds to the shared raft. The page stresses control and coordination, not maximum output.
- **Team synchronization:** The combined result of everyone’s choices. Sharp turns and narrow passages often require the group to act at the same moment.

## Why Coordination Matters More Than Gas Power

The official page treats coordination as a requirement, not a bonus. One player going the wrong way, reacting too late, or farting at the wrong time can send the whole raft off course. That is why the game is framed as a friendship test as much as a physics challenge. The real skill is reading the course and syncing each blast with the team. Courses include narrow passages, sharp turns, moving obstacles, and hazards that throw the raft off balance. Sometimes the group needs to speed up; sometimes it needs to slow down; sometimes everyone must make the right move at exactly the same time.

## Raft Physics: “Sort of Realistic” Arcade Behavior

The developers describe the raft physics as “sort of realistic” and explicitly avoid claiming a perfect real-life simulation. Mechanically, the important point is that the raft reacts to the force, direction, and timing of each player. That can lead to arguments about why the raft suddenly flew a certain way—and, according to the page, some surprisingly scientific discussions.
