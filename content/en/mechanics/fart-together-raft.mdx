The Fart Together raft doesn't have a steering wheel — it has four players, a shared physics body, and a whole lot of gas. That single design choice is why the game swings between perfectly executed turns and total chaos, and it's the mechanic you have to understand before you can clear a course. Here's how the raft actually responds, what the courses throw at you, and what's confirmed about the demo versus the full release.

## How the Fart Together Raft Actually Moves

According to the game's [official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5208630/Fart_Together_Demo/), Fart Together is a physics-based co-op obstacle course game in which **2–4 players steer the same raft using fart power**. Every player contributes force to one shared object, which means the raft has no independent steering — it only goes where the combined inputs push it.

Developer GamesMrkt describes the raft as reacting to three things: the **force**, **direction**, and **timing** of each player's input. The studio is upfront that this isn't a rigorous simulation of real raft physics. What matters is that the results feel consistent enough to argue about — and the studio notes that players often end up having surprisingly scientific discussions about why the raft suddenly shot off in a given direction.

The practical takeaway: you are not controlling a boat. You are one of several thrusters on a boat.

## The Three Inputs That Decide Every Run

Because the raft is shared, every run comes down to managing the same three variables as a team. The game's own description frames the core skill as coordination rather than raw power — "the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right time."

- **Direction** — which way each player's thrust pushes the raft. One player aiming the wrong way drags the whole group off line.
- **Timing** — when the input lands. A late reaction can turn a clean line into a collision.
- **Gas power** — how much force goes into the push. Too much in a narrow gap is just as bad as too little on a straightaway.
- **Team synchronization** — the studio's stated requirement: sometimes the whole crew has to make the right move at exactly the same moment.

Notice that none of these are solo skills. A single player going the wrong way, reacting too late, or firing at the wrong moment can send the entire raft off course — which is exactly the failure state the game is built around.

## Course Obstacles That Test Your Raft Coordination

The demo's course design is built to punish sloppy communication. GamesMrkt lists four recurring obstacle types:

- **Narrow passages** that demand precision over power
- **Sharp turns** that require the team to redirect a heavy shared object
- **Moving obstacles** that force timed responses
- **Hazards that throw the raft off balance**

Courses also shift their demands mid-run. Sometimes you need to speed up; sometimes you need to slow down; sometimes the only correct answer is a synchronized team move. The studio's framing is that a perfect maneuver can rescue a run at the last second, while one small mistake turns the whole attempt into a comedy. That risk profile is why the game's own marketing warns it "may test your friendships."

## Why One Player Can Wreck the Whole Raft

Fart Together is a coordination game disguised as a party game, and the raft is the reason. Because all players push the same physics object, mistakes don't stay local — they propagate. There's no lane to retreat into and no way to carry a weaker player.

That has two consequences worth planning around:

- **Group composition matters.** The store page jokes that a friend with a questionable sense of direction or a habit of saying "What did I do?" will drive the team crazy. In practice, a group that talks constantly will outperform a group of individually skilled players.
- **Blame is a mechanic.** The game tracks the raft's fate, not the argument that followed. Expect short, loud runs and immediate retries.

If you're playing with friends who don't communicate, this is the wrong raft for them.

## Tips for Cleaner Raft Runs

The source material doesn't publish a move list, but the described mechanics point to a few reliable habits:

1. **Call your turns out loud before you take them.** With one shared physics body, a turn that half the team doesn't expect becomes a spin.
2. **Slow down in narrow passages.** The course list explicitly includes tight gaps, and excess gas power is the easiest way to clip a wall.
3. **Assign a caller.** One voice setting the timing for synchronized pushes removes the "I thought you were going" problem.
4. **Treat a flip as data, not a loss.** Runs are short, and the raft's reaction tells you how much force that obstacle actually needed.
5. **Pick your crew deliberately.** The studio's own warning is that coordination is a requirement, not a suggestion.

Fart Together was built by GamesMrkt, an independent studio that has been developing games since 2018; the team says the first version of this one came together over two intense months of tuning raft physics and testing "hippo farts." Whether the full release expands the course set beyond the demo isn't detailed in the store material — so treat the demo as the confirmed picture of the raft mechanics for now.

Two to four players, all steering the same raft in online co-op. Every player's input applies force to one shared physics object.

**Is the Fart Together raft hard to control?**
It's deliberately unstable. The raft reacts to each player's force, direction, and timing, so a single mistimed or misdirected push can send the whole group off course — especially through narrow passages and sharp turns.
