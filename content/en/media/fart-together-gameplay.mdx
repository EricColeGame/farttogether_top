## What Is Fart Together?

Fart Together is a physics-based co-op obstacle course game from indie studio GamesMrkt, and its central joke is also its central mechanic: two to four players share a single raft and steer it by farting. That's the whole pitch, and the store page leans into it hard.

The game is scheduled to launch into Early Access on October 29, 2026, and it's GamesMrkt's first Steam release — the studio says it has been building games since 2018 for other platforms. If you're trying to figure out whether this is a party gag or an actual coordination challenge, the answer is a bit of both.

The [official Fart Together page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/) describes it as a co-op game where direction, timing, and gas power all matter. Steam's user tags back that up, grouping it with party games, comedy, physics, and team-based multiplayer.

## How Fart Together Gameplay Actually Works

Strip away the premise and Fart Together gameplay is a shared-control physics puzzle. Every player's input feeds into the same raft, so the vessel reacts to the combined force, direction, and timing of everyone aboard. Nobody gets a private vehicle.

That design choice creates the friction the game is built around. According to the developers, one player pushing the wrong way, reacting a beat late, or firing at the wrong moment can push the whole raft off course. The store page's own summary of the strategy is blunt: the trick isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right time.

Key elements of the loop:

- **Shared steering** — 2–4 players influence one raft rather than controlling separate craft.
- **Timing over force** — coordinated bursts matter more than raw power.
- **Momentum management** — the raft responds to physics, so course corrections take planning.
- **Constant interdependence** — every input affects the entire team, which is where the comedy and the arguments come from.

The developers are upfront that the simulation isn't realistic. They describe it as "sort of realistic" and joke that arguing about why the raft suddenly veered off may lead to surprisingly scientific conversations. Treat it as slapstick physics, not a nautical simulator.

## Courses, Obstacles, and Difficulty

The course design exists to stress-test teamwork. GamesMrkt lists narrow passages, sharp turns, moving obstacles, and hazards that knock the raft off balance. Some sections demand acceleration, others require slowing down, and some require the entire crew to commit to the same maneuver simultaneously.

That creates a natural difficulty curve built on communication rather than reflexes. A clean, well-timed callout can rescue a run at the last second, while one mistimed burst turns the attempt into a disaster worth clipping. Because the game is in Early Access, expect course layouts and balance to shift as the studio collects feedback.

## Demo, Early Access Plans, and System Specs

A playable demo is available through Steam, which is the most reliable way to judge whether the shared-raft concept clicks with your group before spending money. Feedback is being collected through Steam discussions, the studio's Discord community, and player surveys.
