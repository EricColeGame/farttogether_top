## Fart Together Remote Play: What the Steam Page Actually Confirms

Fart Together is a physics-based co-op game where 2–4 players steer a single shared raft using fart power, and if your crew is scattered across different cities, the setup question matters more than the jokes. The Steam store page confirms **Online Co-op** and a **stable internet connection requirement** — but it does not list Steam's Remote Play Together feature. That distinction is the whole ballgame: the verified way to play apart is the game's built-in online co-op, not game streaming.

This Fart Together remote play guide sticks strictly to what GamesMrkt's store listing shows as of October 29, 2026, and clearly flags what's still unknown so you don't plan a game night around a feature that may not exist.

### Confirmed by the developer's Steam listing

- **Player count:** 2–4 players, all steering the same raft.
- **Mode:** Online co-op, listed under the game's feature row.
- **Connection:** A stable internet connection is required for online co-op — this note appears in both the minimum and recommended system requirements.
- **Other Steam features:** Steam Cloud and Family Sharing.
- **Status:** Early Access, with the planned release date of October 29, 2026.

### Not confirmed

- **Steam Remote Play Together** is not listed among the game's features. Valve's streaming feature lets one player host a local multiplayer game and pipe it to friends, but there is no indication on the store page that Fart Together uses it.
- **Local or split-screen co-op** is not mentioned anywhere in the listing.
- **Steam Deck compatibility** is marked "Unknown."

If Remote Play Together support appears later, it will show up in the features row on the [official Fart Together Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/) — that's the page to re-check before you commit an evening to it.

## How to Prepare for an Online Co-Op Session

The store page doesn't publish step-by-step invite instructions, so treat any specific menu walkthrough you see elsewhere with suspicion. What you *can* do is get everything on your side ready before the raft launches.

- **Confirm everyone is on the same build.** The game is in Early Access, and GamesMrkt has said updates will roll out during development. Mismatched versions are a common cause of failed joins in any online game.
- **Verify the platform.** The listed system requirements are Windows 10 and Windows 11, 64-bit. No other operating system is mentioned.
- **Check the connection, not just the PC.** Broadband internet is a stated requirement for online co-op, separate from the hardware specs.
- **Sort out input.** The listing supports Xbox and PlayStation controllers, and the accessibility features include a "Keyboard Only Option."
- **Grab the demo.** A "Download Fart Together Demo" button appears on the store page — a low-stakes way to confirm the game runs and feels right before organizing a full crew.

## Steam Features That Matter When You're Playing Apart

Three features are listed on the store page, and they do different jobs:

- **Online Co-op** — the actual mechanism for playing with friends remotely.
- **Steam Cloud** — syncs your progress across machines, which helps if your group plays from different setups on different nights.
- **Family Sharing** — a library-sharing feature, not a workaround for getting four friends into one session. Don't plan a co-op night assuming one purchased copy covers the group.

One more practical note: the game supports **17 interface languages**, including English, French, German, Spanish (Spain), Japanese, Korean, Polish, Russian, Turkish, and Simplified Chinese, among others. Interface only — full audio and subtitles aren't listed. If your group spans regions, agree on a common language for callouts before you start, because the game leans hard on real-time coordination.

## Coordination Tips When You're Not in the Same Room

Fart Together's own description is unusually blunt about this: coordination isn't a suggestion, it's a requirement. One player farting at the wrong moment can send the whole raft off course. Playing remotely removes the body language you'd normally rely on, so compensate deliberately.

- **Assign a caller.** One person verbally sets the direction and the count for simultaneous inputs. The game's core idea is farting together at the right time, not farting harder.
- **Talk through the approach before the obstacle.** The courses feature narrow passages, sharp turns, and moving hazards that knock the raft off balance. Deciding "slow down here" out loud beats discovering it mid-collision.
- **Expect the physics to misbehave.** GamesMrkt openly says the raft physics are only "sort of realistic." Treat wild launches as part of the comedy rather than a bug report.
- **Keep the group small while learning.** With 2–4 players, a two-person crew gives you a cleaner read on how each input moves the raft before you add chaos.
