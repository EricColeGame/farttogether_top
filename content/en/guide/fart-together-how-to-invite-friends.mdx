## What the Steam Page Actually Confirms About Multiplayer

If you're trying to figure out **Fart Together how to invite friends**, here's the short answer: the game is built for **2–4 player online co-op**, and invites run through Steam rather than a separate account system. The [official Fart Together Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/) lists Online Co-Op as a core feature and states plainly that "a stable internet connection is required for online co-op."

What the store page does *not* do is walk through the invite menu step by step. So this guide separates two things clearly: what GamesMrkt has confirmed about the game, and what is standard Steam platform behavior you'll likely see when the game goes live.

## Before You Send a Single Invite

Nothing kills a co-op night faster than discovering halfway through that someone can't join. Knock these out first.

- **Everyone needs the game.** Only one person needs to own it for Family Sharing scenarios, but shared libraries generally can't be played simultaneously by two people — plan on separate copies for a 4-player session.
- **Confirm the player count.** Fart Together is designed for 2–4 players steering one shared raft. Solo practice won't replicate the experience, and a fifth friend has nowhere to sit.
- **Check the connection.** The Steam listing specifically calls out a stable broadband connection for online co-op. Wired connections for at least the host reduce the "why did the raft flip" arguments.
- **Verify the platform.** The game lists Windows 10/11 64-bit in its system requirements, and Steam Deck compatibility is marked "Unknown" as of launch.
- **Update Steam.** Invites and the overlay are Steam features, so an outdated client is a common culprit for missing invitations.

## How to Invite Friends to Fart Together on Steam

GamesMrkt hasn't published an official invite walkthrough, so treat the following as the standard Steam co-op flow rather than a verified menu-by-menu transcript. It's the same pattern used across Steam's online multiplayer catalog.

1. **Launch Fart Together and get to the lobby or main menu.** Most co-op titles place host/join options there.
2. **Open the Steam overlay** with Shift+Tab while the game is running.
3. **Open your Friends list** from the overlay.
4. **Find the friend you want and select "Invite to Game."** This option only appears when you're in a joinable state, so if it's grayed out, you're likely still in a menu that doesn't accept players yet.
5. **Have them accept the invite** through the Steam notification that pops up on their end.
6. **Repeat for the rest of your group** until you hit four players.

If the game instead presents a lobby code or a "copy invite link" button, use that — some Steam co-op games prefer codes over the overlay. Follow whatever the on-screen prompt says once you're actually in the lobby.

### Alternative: Join Through the Friends List

Your friends can also invite themselves in. Right-click your name in their Friends list and look for **"Join Game"** while you're hosting. This is often faster than sending invites one at a time, and it's the method most groups default to after the first session.

## Troubleshooting When Invites Don't Land

Invite problems are usually Steam-side, not game-side. Work through these in order.

- **Restart Steam on both machines.** It sounds lazy; it fixes the majority of overlay and presence bugs.
- **Check your online status.** If you're set to Invisible or Offline, friends can't see you as joinable.
- **Confirm the game is actually running.** "Join Game" only appears when the host has the title open and is in a joinable state.
- **Look at region and matchmaking settings.** If the game offers region filters, put everyone on the same one.
- **Test with the demo.** Since a Fart Together demo exists on Steam, a quick two-player demo session is a low-stakes way to confirm invites work before committing a full group.

## Set Ground Rules Before the Raft Moves

Inviting people is the easy part. Keeping four people synchronized on one physics-driven raft is where friendships get tested — GamesMrkt's own store description warns the game "may test your friendships" and notes that one player farting at the wrong moment can send the whole raft off course.

A few practical habits for your first session:

- **Assign a caller.** One person counts down ("three, two, one, go") so thrusts land together.
- **Practice straight lines first.** Get the group moving in one direction before attempting narrow passages or sharp turns.
- **Expect chaos on obstacles.** Moving hazards and tight gaps demand timing over raw power, and the store page is explicit that the secret is farting *together*, not harder.
- **Keep the group small at first.** Two players is a much easier teaching environment than four.
