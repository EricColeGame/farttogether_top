## What Fart Together Actually Asks of You

Fart Together is a physics-based obstacle course game from developer and publisher GamesMrkt, and the whole premise fits in one sentence: two to four players steer a single shared raft using fart power. That means "how to play" isn't really about memorizing a combo list — it's about coordinating direction, timing, and gas output with other people in real time. The game is scheduled to enter Early Access on Steam on October 29, 2026, and the store page frames the core lesson bluntly: the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right moment.

That single design choice shapes everything else. Because every player's input pushes the same raft, one person reacting late or blasting at the wrong angle can drag the whole team off course. If you're looking for a solo skill grind, this isn't it. If you want a party game where communication is the actual mechanic, keep reading.

The store page is the most reliable source for what you need before your first run, so start there rather than with secondhand tips.

## How to Play Fart Together With Friends, Step by Step

**1. Load in together.** Since the raft is shared, everyone needs to be in the same session before the course starts. Use the demo first if your group is unsure about connection quality — online co-op depends on a stable link for all players.

**2. Confirm your controls in-game.** The Steam listing does not publish a keybind chart, so the exact inputs for steering and releasing gas are confirmed inside the game itself. Check the options menu or the demo's opening moments before you blame a friend for a bad turn.

**3. Agree on a direction caller.** The store page warns that one player going the wrong way can send the raft off course. Nominating a single voice to call left, right, faster, or slower removes most of the chaos before it starts.

**4. Sync your timing, not your effort.** Coordination is described as a requirement rather than a suggestion. Players should fire together on a count or a shared cue instead of mashing independently.

**5. Talk constantly.** The game's own marketing leans on the idea that it may test friendships. Treat that as a design warning: the groups that succeed are the ones narrating what they're about to do.

## Raft Physics and Gas Power, Explained

GamesMrkt describes the raft as reacting to the force, direction, and timing of each player — and openly admits it isn't a faithful simulation of real raft physics. What matters practically is that your fart has three dials: which way it pushes, when it fires, and how much thrust it delivers.

Because all three interact across every player at once, the raft's response can feel unpredictable until your group builds a rhythm. Expect the early runs to end with everyone asking why the raft suddenly flew sideways. That's normal. The fix isn't more force; it's tighter timing and cleaner agreement on direction.

One useful habit: after a wipe, name the specific mistake out loud — "we fired late on the turn" — instead of a vague "what happened?" Specific feedback is what turns a chaotic run into a repeatable maneuver.

## Course Tips: Turns, Gaps, and Moving Obstacles

The obstacle courses are built from narrow passages, sharp turns, moving obstacles, and hazards that knock the raft off balance. Each demands a different team response:

- **Narrow passages:** Slow down and keep the raft centered. Extra thrust here usually means a wall.
- **Sharp turns:** Set up early. The turn is won before you reach it, by lining up direction ahead of time.
- **Moving obstacles:** Watch the pattern together and cross on a shared count rather than one at a time.
- **Balance hazards:** After a hit, stabilize before accelerating again. Panic-farting while off balance tends to compound the problem.

The developers note that some sections require speeding up, others require slowing down, and some demand the entire team move at exactly the same instant. A clean maneuver can rescue a run at the last second; a single mistimed input can turn it into a comedy.
