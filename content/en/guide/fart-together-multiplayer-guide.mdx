## Why Fart Together Multiplayer Is Really a Coordination Test

Fart Together puts two to four players on one shared raft and gives the whole group a single propulsion system, so the hardest obstacle in most runs is usually each other. The developer's own store page states the design philosophy plainly: the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right time. That single sentence is the whole guide — everything below is what the official material confirms about multiplayer, and how to turn that rule into repeatable runs.

## How to Get a 2–4 Player Session Running

There's no published lobby walkthrough yet, so keep setup boring and predictable:

- **Confirm everyone clears the system requirements.** The minimum spec calls for 8 GB of RAM and a GTX 1050 Ti or RX 560; the recommended spec doubles memory to 16 GB. All players need a stable connection, not just the host.
- **Try the demo first.** Steam lists a downloadable Fart Together demo. The store page doesn't state whether the demo includes online co-op, so verify that before you rally three friends for a night.

- **Test controllers early.** Xbox and PlayStation pads are both listed as supported, and a keyboard-only option exists for players who prefer it. Mixed input groups should confirm everything registers before a serious attempt.

## The Three Inputs That Decide Every Run

The raft responds to three things per player: **force, direction, and timing**. That's the entire skill ceiling in one line.

- **Direction** is where a player's contribution pushes the raft. One person angled the wrong way drags the whole group off line, which is why the store page warns that a single bad read can send everyone off course.
- **Timing** is when that push lands. A correct input half a second late is a wrong input.
- **Gas power** is how much force goes into the maneuver. More isn't better — the developer is explicit that brute force isn't the answer.

Because all three stack on one shared body, your group is effectively piloting a single clumsy vehicle with four sets of hands on the wheel. Practice runs where nobody tries to "win" are genuinely useful here.

## Handling Narrow Passages, Sharp Turns, and Moving Hazards

The course design, per the store page, leans on four pressure points: narrow passages, sharp turns, moving obstacles, and hazards that knock the raft off balance. The stated rhythm is that some sections demand acceleration, others demand restraint, and some require the entire team to commit on the same beat.

Practical approaches that follow from that design:

1. **Nominate one caller.** A single voice saying "hold," "now," or "left side only" prevents four people from talking over each other during a tight gap.
2. **Slow before you aim.** Straightening the raft ahead of a narrow passage is cheaper than correcting mid-gap, since a hazard hit at an angle tends to compound.
3. **Reset instead of scrambling.** If the raft is already off balance, a coordinated recovery beats four players improvising in different directions.
4. **Run the same course twice.** The first attempt teaches the layout; the second is where coordination actually improves.

## Communication Habits That Save the Friendship

The store page jokes that Fart Together "may test your friendships," and it's not subtle about why: someone reacting late, drifting the wrong way, or asking "what did I do?" can unravel a clean run. A few habits keep the mood intact.

- **Call intent, not blame.** "I'm pushing right" is useful; "you ruined it" is not.
- **Keep callouts to two or three words.** Long explanations arrive after the obstacle.
- **Rotate who calls the shots.** Everyone eventually makes the mistake that ends a run, and shared turns keep it funny instead of tense.
- **Debrief in one sentence.** Name the mistake, then requeue.

Since the game is brand new, no user reviews were posted on Steam at the time of writing, so there's no community consensus on advanced tech or dominant strategies yet. Treat the habits above as starting points and let your own group's runs refine them.
