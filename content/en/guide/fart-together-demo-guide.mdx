## What the Fart Together Demo Actually Is

The Fart Together demo is a free Steam build of a physics-based co-op obstacle course game in which two to four players steer a single raft using fart power. It matters because the demo is the only way to test the game's central promise before buying: whether your group can actually coordinate direction, timing, and gas output without flipping the raft. Steam lists the demo as released on October 8, 2026, developed and published by GamesMrkt, and lists the full game for release on October 29, 2026.

According to the developer's own store description, every player's input affects the whole team. Sharp turns, narrow passages, and moving obstacles all demand that the group think together and act at the same moment. The studio's framing is blunt about the design intent: the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right time.

Confirmed demo facts from the [Fart Together Demo page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5208630/Fart_Together_Demo/):

## How to Get the Demo Running

The demo installs through Steam like any other store build. There is no separate launcher or sign-up flow described on the store page.

1. Open the Fart Together Demo store page in Steam (desktop app or browser).
2. Use the "Play Fart Together Demo" / "Install Demo" button on the page.
3. Let Steam download the roughly 4 GB install.
4. Launch the demo and invite friends for online co-op — the store lists online interactivity as an included element.

A stable broadband connection is required for online co-op, per the developer's system requirements notes. If you plan to play solo first to learn the raft's handling, note that the game is built around a 2–4 player group, so the demo's intended experience is cooperative.

## Co-Op Coordination: What the Demo Trains You For

GamesMrkt describes the raft as reacting to the force, direction, and timing of each player — and openly admits it isn't a faithful simulation of real raft physics. That honesty is useful: treat the raft as a shared, twitchy object rather than something you can muscle through.

The store page's own breakdown of the challenge points to three skills worth practicing in the demo:

- **Shared direction.** One player pushing the wrong way can drag the whole raft off course, so the group needs a common read of where "forward" is at any moment.
- **Timing over power.** The developer explicitly says the answer is simultaneous action, not maximum gas. Practicing synced inputs matters more than spamming.
- **Speed control.** Courses reportedly require the team to speed up sometimes and slow down other times, plus the occasional all-at-once maneuver.

The studio also warns that the game may test friendships, since a single late reaction or mistimed input can end a run. That's marketing copy, not a measured difficulty claim — but it does describe the failure state you'll see in the demo.

## System Requirements and Performance Notes

The demo's requirements are modest by current standards, and they're identical in storage footprint across both tiers.

**Minimum:** Windows 10 64-bit, Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 3 1200, 8 GB RAM, NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti or AMD Radeon RX 560, DirectX 11, 4 GB storage, broadband internet.

**Recommended:** Windows 10/11 64-bit, Intel Core i5-10400 or AMD Ryzen 5 3600, 16 GB RAM, NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660 or AMD Radeon RX 590, DirectX 11, 4 GB storage, broadband internet.

Steam Deck compatibility is listed as **Unknown**, so don't assume handheld support. On the accessibility side, the store page lists camera comfort options, custom volume controls, stereo sound, a keyboard-only option, and playability without quick-time events. Interface text is available in English plus 16 additional languages; the store table shows no full audio or subtitle tracks for any language.
