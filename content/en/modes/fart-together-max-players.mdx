There is no verified five-player or larger mode in the available reference material, so groups planning a session should build around four players.

The key detail is that this is not a game where each player controls a separate raft. The Steam description says players steer the same raft using fart power, with direction, timing, and gas power affecting the team’s movement. The player count is therefore tied directly to how many inputs are acting on one physics object.

## Why Four Players Makes the Shared Raft Harder to Control

With two players, the raft has fewer competing inputs. With four, the team has more people who can push at the wrong moment, react late, or send the raft off course. The official description warns that one player going the wrong way or farting at the wrong time can affect the entire team.

That is the point of the co-op design. The game asks players to think together and act at the right moment, especially when navigating sharp turns, narrow passages, and moving obstacles. Four players do not get four separate lanes; they get one raft and a harder coordination problem.

The Steam page also notes that the raft reacts to each player’s force, direction, and timing. More players can mean more chaotic physics—and more chances for a perfectly timed group move to save a run.

Fart Together is built around the idea that the team succeeds or fails together. A four-player group should treat the raft as one shared control problem, not four separate players doing their own thing.
