## What Is Fart Together on Steam?

Fart Together is a physics-based co-op party game from indie studio GamesMrkt, and it lands on Steam in Early Access on October 29, 2026. Two to four players share a single raft and steer it by farting — coordinating direction, timing, and gas power to survive obstacle courses. If you're searching for the store page and wondering whether it's worth a wishlist slot, the answer depends on one thing: whether your friend group can actually agree on a direction under pressure.

The [official Fart Together page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5117550/Fart_Together/) lists GamesMrkt as both developer and publisher. The studio describes itself as a small independent team that has been building games since 2018, with Fart Together as its first Steam release. The first playable version was built over roughly two months of development, according to the developer's own note on the store page.

A free demo is also offered through Steam, which is the safest way to check performance and controls before committing.

## How the Shared-Raft Co-Op Actually Works

Every player stands on the same raft, so every input affects the whole team. The developer's framing is blunt: the secret isn't farting harder, it's farting together at the right moment. Courses are built around narrow passages, sharp turns, moving obstacles, and hazards designed to knock the raft off balance.

That design creates two very different failure modes. One player can send the raft veering off course with a mistimed burst, or the entire group can hesitate and drift into a hazard. The developers lean into the social fallout, warning that the game "may test your friendships" and that a calm group can end up questioning physics, directions, and each other's abilities within seconds.

On the physics themselves, GamesMrkt is refreshingly candid — they explicitly do not claim a realistic raft simulation. What they do claim is that the raft responds to each player's force, direction, and timing, which produces arguments that accidentally sound scientific. Treat it as party-game physics tuned for comedy and coordination, not a simulator.

Because the game is online co-op, a stable broadband connection is required even though the action is simple.

## What the Roadmap Promises After Early Access

The developer's Early Access statement outlines where the full version is headed: more courses, more obstacles, additional game modes, new characters, and customization options, alongside balance, performance, and polish work. Those are stated intentions rather than dated commitments, and GamesMrkt notes priorities may evolve with feedback.

Community involvement runs through Steam discussion boards, the studio's Discord, and player surveys. Bug reports and suggestions go to the discussion boards, and the team says it plans to share development updates publicly. If you buy in during Early Access, that feedback loop is effectively part of the product — the two-to-three-month window is short, so changes could arrive quickly.

One more detail from the developer's note, offered without further explanation: the original idea for the game came from the toilet. The studio declined to elaborate, which is probably for the best.
