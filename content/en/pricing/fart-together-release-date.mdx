## Fart Together Release Date: The Confirmed Answer

Fart Together launches in **Early Access on October 29, 2026**, on PC via Steam. That date appears twice on the official store listing — once in the release field and once in the "Planned Release Date" notice — so there's no ambiguity about the day itself.

Why does it matter? Because this is an Early Access launch, not a finished 1.0 release, and that changes what you're paying for. The developer, GamesMrkt, has stated plainly that the price during Early Access will be **lower than the eventual full-release price**, and that anyone who buys in early gets the finished version at no extra cost. That makes the launch window itself the pricing story.

As of publication on October 29, 2026, the store page still showed a countdown rather than a live purchase button, indicating the game had not yet unlocked at the time the page was captured.

## What GamesMrkt Has Said About Early Access Pricing

The developer's Early Access FAQ answers the money question directly, and it's the most reliable pricing information available right now:

- **Early Access will be cheaper.** GamesMrkt says it plans to "offer a lower price during Early Access and increase it for the full release as content and features expand."
- **Early buyers are covered.** Anyone who purchases during Early Access "will receive the full release at no additional cost."
- **The increase is tied to content.** The stated rationale is that the 1.0 version will have more courses, obstacles, game modes, characters, and customization options.

In practical terms, that's the standard Early Access value proposition: pay less now, accept an unfinished game, and get the finished one later. What it does *not* tell you is an actual dollar amount, a regional price, or whether a launch discount will apply. None of that was published in the material reviewed.

If you're weighing a day-one purchase, the honest framing is this: you're buying an incomplete build at an unspecified discount, with a two-to-three-month roadmap to a fuller version. That's a reasonable bet if you have a regular group of two to four players ready to jump in immediately — and a much weaker bet if you'd be playing solo or waiting for polish.

## What's Actually in the Early Access Version

GamesMrkt has been upfront that the launch build is a starting point. Their FAQ lists what the full version is planned to add: more courses, more obstacles, additional game modes, new characters, and expanded customization, plus improvements to balance, performance, and overall polish.

The core loop at launch is described on the store page as physics-based obstacle racing for two to four players sharing a single raft. Each player contributes force, direction, and timing, so the raft's movement is a group effort rather than individual control. Courses include narrow passages, sharp turns, moving obstacles, and hazards designed to knock the raft off balance.

The developer also notes that plans and priorities "may evolve" with player feedback — a standard caveat, but a real one. Early Access roadmaps are intentions, not contracts.

## Is There a Demo Before You Buy?

Yes. A **free demo** is available through the Steam page, and the store listing includes a dedicated demo download section. If the missing price tag is the thing holding you back, the demo is the obvious way to test whether the raft physics and the coordination chaos actually land with your group.

Two practical points before you install:

- The game requires a stable internet connection for online co-op, per the system requirements.
- Minimum specs call for a 64-bit Windows 10 system, 8 GB of RAM, and a GTX 1050 Ti or RX 560-class GPU, with 4 GB of storage. Recommended specs step up to 16 GB of RAM and a GTX 1660 or RX 590.

Interface support covers English plus 16 additional languages, including French, German, Spanish, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, and Brazilian Portuguese. Note that the language table shows interface translation only — no full audio or subtitle columns were marked.

GamesMrkt describes itself as an independent studio developing games since 2018, with Fart Together as its first Steam release — built, by their account, over roughly two months. That context matters for pricing patience: a small team's first Steam launch is exactly the kind of release where dates and prices can shift.
